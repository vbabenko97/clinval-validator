# Deck patterns

Choose slide archetypes based on communication need. Do not use every pattern just because it exists. That is how slide decks become landfill with transitions.

## Opening patterns

### Title slide
Use for deck identity, audience, date, presenter, and optional subtitle. Keep it clean and confident.

### Executive summary
Use when decision-makers need the answer first. Structure as 3-5 claims with implications or recommendations.

### Agenda
Use when the session is long, interactive, or multi-section. Skip it for short persuasive decks unless navigation matters.

### Context / why now
Use to establish urgency, market shift, research gap, operational pain, or decision pressure.

## Argument patterns

### Problem framing
Use to define the pain, scope, affected users, costs, and consequences. Good for strategy, product, research, and sales decks.

### Insight slide
Use to reveal the non-obvious finding. Lead with the insight, then support it with data, quote, diagram, or example.

### Recommendation slide
Use when the deck needs a decision. Include recommendation, rationale, tradeoffs, and next step.

### Options comparison
Use for A/B/C decisions. Compare criteria that matter, not random attributes pulled from the void.

### Before / after
Use to show transformation, redesign, workflow improvement, model performance improvement, or operational change.

## Evidence patterns

### Data chart
Use for quantitative proof. Choose the chart type based on the relationship: trend, comparison, composition, distribution, correlation.

### Research method
Use for academic, scientific, or technical decks. Show cohort/data, method pipeline, validation design, and limitations.

### Finding card grid
Use for multiple insights of equal weight. Keep cards parallel and concise.

### Case study
Use to show a concrete example. Structure: context, intervention, result, lesson.

### Quote / voice of customer
Use for qualitative evidence. Keep the quote short and pair it with implication.

## Explanation patterns

### Process flow
Use for workflows, pipelines, operations, handoffs, or methodology. Show direction and ownership clearly.

### Architecture diagram
Use for systems, platforms, GenAI pipelines, data flows, or infrastructure. Separate layers, show inputs/outputs, and avoid spaghetti arrows.

### Concept diagram
Use to explain a model, framework, or relationship. Prefer simple shapes and labels over decorative complexity.

### Timeline / roadmap
Use for sequencing. Show phases, milestones, dependencies, and decision gates.

## Product and GTM patterns

### Value proposition
Use for product, sales, or investor decks. Link audience pain to differentiated value.

### Feature workflow
Use to show how the user experiences the product. Prefer a 3-5 step flow with screenshots or simplified UI states.

### Market / customer segment
Use to define target users, segmentation, or prioritization.

### Pricing / packaging
Use for commercial decisions. Keep tiers readable and emphasize the recommended package.

## Closing patterns

### Risks and mitigations
Use when credibility matters. Pair each risk with mitigation and owner.

### Implementation plan
Use to turn strategy into action. Include phases, owners, timeline, and measurable outcomes.

### Decision slide
Use at the end of executive decks. State the requested decision and what happens next.

### Appendix divider
Use to separate backup evidence from the main story.

## Matching patterns to common requests

- investor pitch: title, problem, market, solution, product workflow, traction, business model, GTM, team, ask
- executive strategy: title, executive summary, context, diagnosis, options, recommendation, roadmap, risks, decision
- research presentation: title, research question, method, cohort/data, results, interpretation, limitations, conclusion
- product update: title, progress summary, goals, shipped work, metrics, blockers, roadmap, asks
- technical architecture: title, context, requirements, architecture, data flow, tradeoffs, risks, rollout plan
- training deck: title, objectives, concept, example, activity, checklist, recap
