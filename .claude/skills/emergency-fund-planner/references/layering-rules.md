# Emergency Fund Layering Rules

Use this reference when a user asks for storage structure, currency split, or risk review.

## Design priority

1. Availability during real emergencies.
2. Capital stability.
3. Counterparty diversification.
4. Currency fit.
5. Yield.

Yield never outranks access. A product that pays more but cannot be used during a crisis is not emergency storage; it is financial cosplay with a login screen.

## Layer defaults

Layer 0: physical cash, 1 to 2 weeks of mandatory expenses.

Layer 1: instant-access bank/card balance, enough to complete the first month of mandatory expenses together with Layer 0.

Layer 2: short-term deposit, no-penalty deposit, money-market-style deposit account, or treasury-bill equivalent where appropriate and accessible. Use this only for the deeper reserve after the first month is covered.

Layer 3: relocation buffer, separate from the emergency fund. Use 1 to 3 months of mandatory expenses unless the user provides a real relocation budget.

Layer 4: long-term investments. This is not part of the emergency fund.

## Maximum share guidance

- Cash: normally 5% to 15% of the emergency target.
- Instant access bank/card: normally 20% to 40% of the emergency target.
- Short-term deposit or T-bill equivalent: normally up to 60% to 75% of the emergency target.
- Relocation buffer: separate target, not capped by emergency-fund share.
- Long-term investments: 0% of the emergency fund.

## Escalation triggers

Increase target months when any of these apply:

- income is variable, contract-based, commission-based, or startup-dependent;
- job search may take more than 3 months;
- user has dependents;
- user has high minimum debt payments;
- user may relocate quickly;
- user faces blackout, war-risk, capital-control, or bank-access disruption scenarios;
- expenses are in a stronger currency than income;
- health or family obligations create low tolerance for cash stress.

## Instruments to reject for emergency storage

Reject stocks, equity ETFs, crypto assets, private equity, venture exposure, long-duration bonds, leveraged funds, locked products with punitive exits, collectibles, and any product whose price or access can fail exactly when the emergency happens.

Short-term sovereign bills or equivalent instruments may be acceptable for Layer 2 only if settlement, currency, issuer risk, tax treatment, and access are suitable for the user's jurisdiction.
