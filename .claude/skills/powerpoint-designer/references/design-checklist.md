# Design checklist

Use this checklist for PowerPoint creation, critique, and redesign. Apply judgment; the goal is a better deck, not a ritual sacrifice to checkboxes.

## Narrative QA

- The deck has a clear audience and purpose.
- The first 1-3 slides establish why the audience should care.
- Each section advances the argument.
- Slide titles communicate claims, not generic labels.
- The recommendation or next action is explicit.
- Evidence appears before or near the claim it supports.
- Caveats are visible where they affect decisions.

## Slide-level clarity

- Each slide has one dominant message.
- The viewer can identify the focal point within 3 seconds.
- Dense slides are split unless density is intentional for reference use.
- Bullets are parallel, short, and grouped.
- Labels and annotations explain charts directly.
- Acronyms are expanded on first use unless the audience certainly knows them.

## Layout and visual hierarchy

- Use a consistent margin and grid.
- Align objects deliberately; do not eyeball alignment like civilization is optional.
- Keep related elements visually grouped.
- Use whitespace as structure.
- Avoid more than 2-3 type sizes per slide.
- Use emphasis sparingly: bold, accent color, callout, or size, not all at once.
- Avoid decorative icons that do not aid comprehension.

## Typography

- Prefer familiar sans-serif fonts for readability.
- Use at least 18 pt body text for normal presentation slides when feasible.
- Avoid all-caps blocks, excessive italics, underlines, and cramped line spacing.
- Keep title, subtitle, body, caption, and footer styles consistent.
- Do not shrink text to solve content overload; edit the content.

## Color and contrast

- Use strong contrast between text and background.
- Use one primary accent color plus limited supporting colors.
- Do not rely on color alone to encode meaning; add labels, shapes, patterns, or text.
- Avoid low-contrast gray text, especially on projectors and video calls.
- For accessibility-oriented decks, target WCAG-style contrast: 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text where practical.

## Charts and data visuals

- Use a takeaway title.
- Label axes and units.
- Remove unnecessary gridlines, shadows, borders, and legends when direct labels work.
- Sort bars meaningfully.
- Use line charts for trends, bars for comparisons, scatterplots for relationships, and tables only for lookup/detail.
- Annotate the important point rather than forcing the audience to perform spreadsheet archaeology.
- State data source and date if relevant.

## Tables

- Avoid tables when a chart, grouped list, or diagram communicates better.
- Use tables only for actual tabular comparison or lookup.
- Keep headers clear.
- Avoid merged cells, nested tables, and tiny text.
- Highlight the important row/column with restraint.

## Images, screenshots, and diagrams

- Crop screenshots to the relevant area.
- Add callouts or overlays that explain what matters.
- Keep diagrams directional and simple.
- Prefer editable shapes for process and architecture diagrams.
- Avoid stretched images, low-resolution screenshots, and irrelevant stock photography.

## Accessibility QA

Based on common PowerPoint and accessibility best practices:

- every slide has a unique descriptive title, visible or hidden
- important visuals include alt text or nearby equivalent explanation
- reading order follows the intended sequence
- contrast is sufficient
- color is not the only carrier of meaning
- hyperlinks are descriptive
- tables are simple and have headers
- audio/video content has captions or descriptions when relevant
- exported PDFs preserve tags when possible

## Production QA before delivery

- All slides use the intended aspect ratio.
- Fonts are consistent and available or safely substituted.
- Objects are aligned and not accidentally off-slide.
- Slide numbers, footers, and section markers are consistent.
- Notes are present only when useful.
- Placeholder text is removed.
- File opens successfully.
- The final deck is editable wherever practical.
