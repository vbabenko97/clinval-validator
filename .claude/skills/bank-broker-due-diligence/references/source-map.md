# Source Map for Bank, Broker, and Platform Due Diligence

Use this reference to choose primary sources. Always prefer the regulator or official protection-scheme page over platform marketing.

## Global source hierarchy

1. Official regulator register or warning list
2. Official investor compensation or deposit guarantee scheme
3. Product legal documents: prospectus, KID/KIID, customer agreement, fee schedule, terms, custody agreement
4. Audited financial statements and annual reports
5. Official tax-document help pages
6. Reputable financial press for controversies, enforcement actions, outages, or insolvency history
7. User reviews and forums only as weak signals, useful for patterns but not proof

## United States

Use for US broker-dealers, advisers, banks, and US-facing investment products:

- FINRA BrokerCheck: broker-dealer firms and individual brokers
- SEC IAPD: registered investment advisers and adviser firms
- SEC EDGAR: public company filings, ETF and fund filings, prospectuses
- Investor.gov: SEC investor education and background-check guidance
- SIPC member list and SIPC investor pages: brokerage customer protection
- FDIC BankFind and FDIC insurance resources: bank deposit insurance
- NCUA Research a Credit Union: credit union insurance
- CFTC/NFA BASIC: futures, forex, commodity pool operators, commodity trading advisers
- State securities regulators when state-level registration matters

Key checks:

- Is the legal entity registered for the exact activity?
- Is the person registered, not merely the firm?
- Are there disclosures, disciplinary events, arbitration awards, or customer complaints?
- Is SIPC membership claimed correctly?
- Are bank deposits FDIC/NCUA insured, or is cash actually swept into something else?

## European Union and EEA

Use for banks, brokers, investment firms, funds, and payment institutions serving EU/EEA users:

- National competent authority registers, such as BaFin, AMF, CONSOB, CNMV, CSSF, AFM, FI, FMA Austria, Bank of Lithuania, Central Bank of Ireland, CySEC, and others
- ESMA registers and warning resources where applicable
- European Commission deposit guarantee scheme information for general EU DGS framework
- National deposit guarantee schemes for exact coverage and eligibility
- Investor compensation schemes for investment firms, which differ from bank deposit schemes
- Fund regulator and fund domicile documents for UCITS, AIFs, ETFs, and money market funds

Key checks:

- Branch or subsidiary status matters. Deposit protection can depend on the home-country scheme.
- Passporting does not always mean the local regulator is the primary supervisor.
- A payments or e-money license is not the same as a banking license.
- UCITS status helps with fund regulation, but it does not remove market risk.
- PRIIPs KID availability can affect retail access.

## United Kingdom

Use for UK firms and UK-facing offers:

- FCA Financial Services Register and FCA Firm Checker
- FCA Warning List for unauthorized or clone firms
- FSCS for compensation eligibility and limits
- PRA where bank authorization is relevant
- Companies House for corporate identity, directors, filings, and solvency signals

Key checks:

- Confirm the firm has permission for the exact product or service.
- Check whether the firm is authorized, registered, no longer authorized, revoked, or only an appointed representative.
- For appointed representatives, verify the principal and permitted activities.
- If a firm is unauthorized for the activity, complaint and compensation protections may not apply.

## Canada

Use for Canadian banks, brokers, advisers, and investment dealers:

- Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization (CIRO) dealer/adviser resources
- Canadian Securities Administrators (CSA) National Registration Search
- Canadian Investor Protection Fund (CIPF) for investment dealer insolvency protection
- Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) for eligible bank deposits
- Provincial securities commissions when relevant

Key checks:

- Confirm registration category and province.
- Confirm whether the dealer is a CIPF member.
- Confirm CDIC eligibility for bank deposits and product type.

## Australia and New Zealand

Australia:

- ASIC Professional Registers and AFSL checks
- APRA register for authorized deposit-taking institutions
- Financial Claims Scheme for eligible deposits

New Zealand:

- Financial Service Providers Register
- Financial Markets Authority warnings and licensing
- Reserve Bank registers for banks and insurers

Key checks:

- Confirm AFSL or equivalent license covers the actual product.
- Confirm whether the provider can legally serve the user's country of residence.

## Crypto, tokenized assets, and high-yield platforms

Use extreme caution. Check:

- Whether the entity is only AML-registered or actually authorized for regulated investment services
- Custody terms and insolvency treatment
- Proof of reserves, but do not treat it as a substitute for audited liabilities and legal protection
- Whether customer assets are segregated or lent out
- Whether yield comes from lending, staking, derivatives, rehypothecation, or unsecured credit exposure
- Whether investor compensation or deposit guarantee schemes explicitly exclude the product

Default stance: if legal status, custody, source of yield, or withdrawal mechanics are unclear, verdict should be `avoid` or `needs human review`.
