# Source Anchors

This skill uses stable principles, not fixed product recommendations.

## Investor.gov principle

Investor.gov's rainy-day guidance says savings are usually held in safe places that allow access to money at any time, including savings accounts, checking accounts, and certificates of deposit. It also notes the tradeoff: safety and availability usually mean low interest, and inflation can reduce purchasing power over time. It says some investors keep up to six months of income in savings for emergencies such as sudden unemployment.

Use this to justify why the emergency fund should be liquid and low-volatility, while also acknowledging inflation drag.

## FDIC principle

FDIC guidance says deposit insurance protects traditional deposit accounts at insured banks, including checking, savings, money market deposit accounts, and CDs, while products such as mutual funds, stocks, bonds, and crypto assets are not covered by FDIC deposit insurance.

Use this as a counterparty-risk pattern. For non-US users, verify the current local deposit-insurance or guarantee regime from official sources before giving exact limits.

## Current-data rule

Always browse or use a current trusted source before stating:

- deposit-insurance limits;
- bank solvency or access claims;
- current deposit, CD, treasury-bill, or money-market rates;
- tax treatment;
- capital controls;
- war-time banking restrictions;
- country-specific rules for cross-border transfers or cash movement.
