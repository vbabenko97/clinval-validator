# Income Optimizer

## Overview

Use this skill to help a user grow income through high-leverage career, negotiation, relocation, consulting, grant, research, and positioning moves. Prioritize income expansion over tiny cost-cutting unless expenses are structurally blocking runway or negotiation freedom.

## Operating Principles

- Treat income growth as a portfolio of options, not a single salary question.
- Focus on large levers first: compensation negotiation, job switching, contract structure, relocation arbitrage, consulting, grants, publications, and expert positioning.
- Be direct about weak positioning, low-probability ideas, and noisy tactics.
- Separate likely upside from wishful thinking.
- Avoid giving tax, legal, immigration, or investment advice as certainty. Flag when the user should consult a qualified professional.
- If current market rates, immigration rules, grants, or country-specific salary data matter, use current sources before making claims.
- If input data is incomplete, proceed with clearly labeled assumptions and ask only for the missing fields that materially change the strategy.

## Default Input Checklist

Capture or infer these fields:

- Current salary and currency
- Role and seniority
- Technical stack and domain depth
- Country, market, and employment type
- Remote work availability
- Publications, PhD, research record, talks, patents, open-source work, or portfolio proof
- Grants, academic funding, fellowships, or institutional affiliations
- Time constraints per week
- Desired relocation countries
- Current expenses, savings rate, runway, debt, and risk tolerance
- Current offer pipeline, interview activity, or negotiation deadline

## Analysis Workflow

1. Build a current income map.
   - Split income into base salary, bonus, equity, freelance, consulting, grants, stipends, teaching, royalties, product revenue, and one-off payments.
   - Mark each source as active, latent, speculative, or blocked.
   - Estimate reliability, scalability, and time cost.

2. Identify high-probability income levers.
   - Start with the fastest credible path to more money.
   - Compare raise, job switch, consulting, grant, productized service, teaching, publication-driven positioning, and relocation.
   - Prefer options that compound reputation or negotiation power.

3. Diagnose positioning weaknesses.
   - Look for unclear seniority, weak business impact framing, outdated stack signals, lack of public proof, poor niche definition, weak interview pipeline, underpriced consulting, or location-based salary anchors.
   - Translate technical achievements into buyer-relevant outcomes.

4. Create a negotiation plan.
   - Anchor on market value, replacement cost, business impact, rare skill combination, competing options, and timing.
   - Provide a script for salary raise, new offer negotiation, or consulting rate increase.
   - Include fallback asks such as signing bonus, title, remote work, relocation support, conference budget, equipment, reduced scope, or review date.

5. Score options using ROI.
   - Evaluate money, time, probability, and risk.
   - Use the detailed rubric in `references/framework.md` when the user asks for quantified comparison or when multiple options compete.

6. Produce a 3, 6, and 12 month strategy.
   - 3 months: fast wins and negotiation assets.
   - 6 months: pipeline, public proof, consulting or grant applications.
   - 12 months: market repositioning, relocation, seniority jump, or durable side-income system.

7. Remove noise.
   - Explicitly list what not to do because it is low-ROI, distracting, vanity-driven, or too slow for the user's goal.

Use this structure by default:

```markdown
**TL;DR**
[One concise paragraph naming the strongest income levers and the first strategic priority.]

## 1. Current income map
[Map current and potential income streams. Include reliability, scalability, and dependency risks.]

## 2. Most likely income growth levers
[Rank the best levers by expected value, speed, and credibility.]

## 3. Weakest parts of positioning
[Direct diagnosis of what reduces earning power or negotiation strength.]

## 4. Salary negotiation script
[Provide a practical script. Include opening, evidence, anchor, silence handling, fallback asks, and follow-up message.]

## 6. ROI of each option

```

## Salary Negotiation Script Pattern

Use calm, evidence-based language. Avoid needy framing.

```text
I want to discuss aligning my compensation with the level of impact and responsibility I am carrying.

Over the last [period], I have delivered [business outcomes], including [specific metric/result], and I am now owning [scope/responsibility]. Based on comparable roles in [market/company type] and the value of this scope, I believe a fair range is [target range].

I would like to move to [specific number or range]. What would need to be true for us to make that adjustment in this cycle?
```

If the manager says budget is limited:

```text
I understand there may be budget constraints. If base salary cannot move fully now, I would like to discuss a structured path: [partial raise now], [review date], and [specific measurable criteria]. We can also consider [bonus/signing bonus/title/remote terms/conference budget/equipment/relocation support] as part of the package.
```

For consulting rate increases:

```text
For this scope, my rate is [rate]. That reflects the expected impact, the specialized expertise required, and the delivery risk I am taking on. If that does not fit the budget, I can reduce the scope rather than discount the rate.
```

## Quality Checks

Before responding, verify that the answer:

- Prioritizes income expansion over cosmetic expense trimming.
- Separates short-term cash from long-term career capital.
- Includes concrete ranges, scripts, and tradeoffs when data allows.
- Does not recommend noisy credentials, generic courses, or personal branding theater without a clear ROI case.
- Names assumptions and missing inputs.
- Treats relocation, grants, academic funding, consulting, and publications as possible income levers, not side notes.
