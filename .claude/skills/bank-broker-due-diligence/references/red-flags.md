# Red Flags and Severity Guide

Use this guide to classify risk. When in doubt, be conservative.

## Critical red flags: usually avoid

- Regulator warning, unauthorized-firm warning, clone-firm warning, or cease-and-desist order
- No verifiable legal entity
- Claimed license cannot be found in the regulator register
- License belongs to a different entity or clone target
- License exists but does not cover the product being sold
- Customer funds sent to an unrelated company or individual account
- Guaranteed high returns, fixed daily yield, or risk-free language for market products
- Withdrawal delays explained only by vague compliance excuses, especially with requests for extra payments
- User must pay tax, commission, unlock fee, or verification fee before withdrawing
- Platform primarily accepts crypto or wire transfers to unrelated beneficiaries
- Terms allow broad unilateral asset liquidation or transfer without clear regulatory protections
- Product is unavailable to the user's country but platform still encourages signup through workarounds

## High red flags: risky or needs human review

- Offshore contracting entity for retail users while marketing local protection
- Complex custody chain with unclear beneficial ownership
- Omnibus account structure without clear insolvency treatment
- No transparent fee schedule before account opening
- Frequent country offboarding, frozen accounts, or KYC lockouts for users with similar residence/citizenship
- Aggressive leverage, CFDs, options, structured notes, crypto staking, or private credit marketed as savings
- No tax reports or only summary reports for a tax-sensitive product
- Poor complaint history from regulators, ombudsman, or arbitration databases
- Recent enforcement, capital adequacy issues, banking partner loss, auditor resignation, or insolvency rumors
- Product depends on a single banking partner, custodian, issuer, or market maker without contingency explanation

## Medium red flags: mitigate or limit exposure

- Fees are high but disclosed
- FX spread is unclear but can be measured from test conversion
- Tax reports exist but are incomplete
- Interface hides legal documents until late onboarding
- Limited withdrawal rails or manual withdrawal process
- Support gives inconsistent answers but official documents are clear
- Small inactivity or transfer fees that matter only for low balances

## Low red flags: monitor

- Minor user-interface complaints
- Occasional app outages without custody or withdrawal impact
- New product line under an established regulated entity
- Customer-service delays during peak periods

## Risk scoring shortcut

Use this shortcut only as an internal reasoning aid:

- Any critical red flag: `avoid`, unless clearly resolved by primary evidence
- Two or more high red flags: `avoid` or `needs human review`
- One high red flag: at least `risky`
- Multiple medium red flags: `risky`
- No high/critical red flags, verified license, appropriate protection, and reasonable fees: possibly `suitable`

Always explain the reason, not just the label.
