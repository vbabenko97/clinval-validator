# Income Optimization Framework

Use this reference when a user asks for a detailed income strategy, option comparison, salary negotiation plan, relocation analysis, consulting strategy, grant strategy, or ROI assessment.

## Income Map Categories

Classify each income source by type and quality.

### Types

- Base salary: recurring employment income.
- Variable compensation: bonus, commission, profit share, overtime, or performance pay.
- Equity or phantom equity: stock options, RSUs, token grants, carried interest, or similar upside instruments.
- Freelance and consulting: hourly, day-rate, fixed-scope, retainer, advisory, or implementation work.
- Research and academic funding: stipend, fellowship, grant salary coverage, teaching, supervision, reviewer honoraria, or institutional funding.
- Productized service: repeatable fixed-scope service with defined deliverables and pricing.
- Product income: course, template, software, dataset, report, API, subscription, or licensing revenue.
- Reputation-driven income: speaking, workshops, expert interviews, expert networks, expert witness work, or paid advisory sessions.

### Quality Dimensions

- Reliability: how predictable the income is.
- Scalability: whether income can grow without a proportional increase in hours.
- Control: how much the user can influence the outcome.
- Time-to-cash: how quickly the option can produce money.
- Career capital: whether the work improves future earning power.
- Downside risk: legal, tax, visa, reputation, burnout, employer conflict, or opportunity cost.

## ROI Scoring

Score each option using these dimensions. Use numbers only when the user's data supports them; otherwise use low, medium, or high with reasoning.

### Money

Estimate realistic incremental annualized upside.

- Low: under 5 percent of current annual income or mostly symbolic.
- Medium: 5 to 20 percent of current annual income.
- High: 20 to 50 percent of current annual income.
- Very high: above 50 percent of current annual income or creates a step-change in market level.

### Time

Estimate total effort before meaningful outcome.

- Low: less than 10 hours.
- Medium: 10 to 40 hours.
- High: 40 to 120 hours.
- Very high: more than 120 hours or multi-month sustained execution.

### Probability

Estimate likelihood of a meaningful win.

- Low: under 25 percent.
- Medium: 25 to 50 percent.
- High: 50 to 75 percent.
- Very high: above 75 percent.

### Risk

Estimate downside if the option fails.

- Low: limited downside, mostly time cost.
- Medium: possible reputation, opportunity, or focus cost.
- High: material financial, employment, visa, legal, or burnout risk.
- Extreme: could seriously damage employment, immigration status, or finances.

## Recommended Option Set

Always consider these options unless clearly irrelevant:

1. Raise at current employer
   - Best when impact is proven, scope increased, and the employer has budget.
   - Weak when the role is capped, company is distressed, or the user has no leverage.

2. Job switch
   - Best when the current market pays materially more for the user's profile.
   - Requires interview pipeline, proof of seniority, and credible alternatives.

3. Consulting
   - Best when the user has a niche buyer pain and can sell outcomes, not hours.
   - Avoid broad generic profiles like "AI consultant" without a concrete business problem.

4. Grant or academic funding
   - Best when publications, institutional affiliation, PhD, or research program fit a funder's priorities.
   - Risk is long timelines and administrative drag.

5. Productized service
   - Best when repeated consulting requests can be packaged into a narrow offer.
   - Examples: LLM evaluation audit, RAG quality audit, radiomics reproducibility audit, grant proposal technical review, medical imaging ML validation review.

6. Relocation or remote market shift
   - Best when salary bands in target countries exceed local compensation enough to cover cost, tax, and visa friction.
   - Must include visa, taxes, cost of living, healthcare, family obligations, and runway constraints.

7. Publication or portfolio strategy
   - Best when the user's market value depends on trust, domain authority, or expert signaling.
   - Weak if it becomes unpaid perfectionism with no buyer or employer conversion path.

## Positioning Diagnosis

Look for these weak spots:

- Role title undersells actual scope.
- Achievements are described as tasks rather than outcomes.
- No quantified impact.
- Stack reads generic instead of market-specific.
- Research record is disconnected from industry value.
- Consulting offer sells labor instead of risk reduction, speed, compliance, or revenue.
- Relocation story lacks target market fit.
- Publications are not translated into credibility for employers, funders, or clients.
- No proof assets: case studies, GitHub, papers, demos, talks, benchmarks, reports, or testimonials.

## Output Guidance for AI Engineer and Data Scientist Profiles

For AI Engineer, Data Scientist, GenAI, MLOps, radiomics, or research-heavy profiles, prioritize:

- Multi-provider LLM systems, evaluation, observability, cost control, reliability, and production hardening.
- Domain specialization that buyers respect, such as medical imaging, radiomics, regulated AI, defense-adjacent research, or UAV operator workload analysis.
- Concrete proof: reproducible pipelines, benchmarks, deployment constraints, papers, grant deliverables, and before/after system metrics.
- Positioning as a rare combination of research depth plus production execution.

Avoid positioning the user as only a prompt engineer, generic data scientist, or vague AI generalist unless the market context demands it.

## Noise Filter

These are usually low-ROI unless tied to a concrete opportunity:

- Random certificates without employer or client demand.
- Unfocused public posting with no niche or buyer.
- Tiny expense cuts that do not change runway or negotiating power.
- Rebuilding the CV endlessly instead of testing the market.
- Applying to hundreds of unfocused jobs.
- Learning another tool only because it is trending.
- Underpriced freelance work that consumes evenings and weakens the main career move.
- Grants with poor fit, weak sponsor support, or timelines that do not match the user's cash needs.

## Strategy Horizon

### 3 Months

Focus on fast leverage:

- Compensation evidence pack.
- Negotiation conversation or offer pipeline.
- Revised positioning and CV.
- One strong public proof asset or case study.
- Consulting offer validation with a narrow target buyer.

### 6 Months

Focus on market expansion:

- Interview pipeline in target markets.
- Consulting pilots or retainers.
- Grant applications with realistic fit.
- Publication or conference submissions that improve credibility.
- Relocation feasibility narrowed to 1 to 3 countries.

### 12 Months

Focus on compounding:

- Seniority or market-band upgrade.
- Relocation or remote-market transition.
- Durable consulting channel or productized service.
- Strong reputation assets.
- Better contract structure, bonus, equity, or institutional funding.
