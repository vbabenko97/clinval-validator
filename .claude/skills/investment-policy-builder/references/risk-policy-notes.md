# Risk Policy Notes

Use these notes when translating user inputs into policy language.

## Risk tolerance versus risk capacity

Risk tolerance is what the user believes they can emotionally handle. Risk capacity is what their finances, obligations, and time horizon can survive. The IPS should obey the more conservative of the two.

## Horizon dominates ambition

Short-horizon, fixed-date goals require capital preservation even for users who describe themselves as aggressive. A user can be aggressive with retirement money and conservative with relocation money at the same time.

## Maximum drawdown is a behavioral circuit breaker

If the user states a maximum tolerable drawdown, treat it as a hard constraint. The IPS should reduce growth allocation, increase cash/fixed income, or separate risky and protected buckets when needed.

## Currency mismatch risk

A portfolio can rise in market terms and still fail a goal if it is held in the wrong currency. For near-term goals, policy should generally match assets to the expected spending currency.

## Simplicity bias

At policy level, prefer broad asset classes and plain-English rules. Avoid complexity until there is a separate instrument due diligence step.
